# How we communicate — gem.studio

The canonical rules and language for the site, the blog, and outreach.
**Read this before writing a single line of public copy.** When Anuj
approves new language, it gets added here. When something is rejected,
the reason gets added to the banned list. This file is the memory.

---

## 1. The rules

1. **Every sentence names an object.** A story element, a dollar figure,
   a stage of a project, a person's record, a budget line, a release
   path. If a sentence would still be true for a company that has none
   of this, cut it.
2. **No summary abstractions.** Banned: "what it can become and what it
   takes to get there", "unlock its potential", "the full picture",
   "at every stage" used alone without saying which stages.
3. **No innuendo about being better.** Do not write "nothing else does
   this", "no one has ever attempted", "that is why we can". State the
   actual capability; the reader draws the conclusion.
4. **No model architecture.** Never explain how it is built (training,
   architecture, machine learning mechanics, percentiles, calibration).
   Say what it does and what it returns.
5. **No storytelling or narrative color.** No "it comes in", no "a
   writer without a name", no dramatized example films with arcs.
6. **No slogans as headings.** Headings are the literal thing:
   "IP acquisition.", "Production financing.", "Cast and crew."
7. **No disclaimers or footnotes** in graphics ("example project",
   "priced at whatever stage"). If it needs a caveat, it is wrong.
8. **No em dashes.** Ever. Use a colon, a comma, or a full stop.
8a. **No incomplete marketing sentences.** Banned forever: a dramatic
   short sentence standing in for substance ("We can.", "That is what
   lets us go after original films.", "That changes the math."). Every
   sentence is complete and carries its own content. No punchy fragment
   endings, no weak "that is what lets us" closers.
9. **Never imply we own or hold film data.** Use "studied",
   "measured", "in our study". Never "our data", "trained on",
   "our dataset".
10. **Detail is the product.** Length is fine when every clause is
    concrete. Compression that removes specifics is a downgrade, not
    an edit.
11. **Contrast and size:** nothing below 13px on canvas, nothing in dim
    gray on the dark ground. Labels attach to objects. Nothing collides.

## 3. Selznick

What Selznick actually does, in the order we say it:

- reads **what is in a story**: what happens, who the characters are,
  how it is built, how rare that combination is against nearly every
  film of the last fifty years
- knows **who is in it**: every credit across a career, what each person
  has delivered on this kind of material and at this budget, who they do
  their best work with, how much attention they carry going into release
- knows **the market** they are going into
- **projects financial performance at any stage** of a project, from a
  screenplay, through development, casting and production, to theatrical
  release, and **each projection is sharper than the last**

Approved paragraph:

> Selznick is the most powerful AI creative partner ever built. It reads
> what is in a story: what happens, who the characters are, how it is
> built, and how rare that combination is against nearly every film of
> the last fifty years. It knows who is in it: every credit across a
> career, what each person has delivered on this kind of material at
> this budget, who they do their best work with, and how much attention
> they carry going into a release. And it knows the market they are
> going into. From that, Selznick projects the financial performance of
> a project at any stage, from a screenplay through development, casting
> and production, to theatrical release, and every projection is sharper
> than the one before it.

## 4. IP acquisition

The point: we can price any IP at any stage, which is what lets us take
on projects the rest of the market overlooks.

Approved paragraph:

> Selznick projects the financial performance and market potential of
> any IP at any stage. Material that needs to be adapted, a screenplay
> we option or buy from a writer and develop into a feature, or a
> completed film looking for distribution: in each case we project what
> it earns, we understand what it costs, and we make the investment with
> a precision the market cannot match. That precision is what gives us
> the appetite to take on projects everyone else in Hollywood overlooks
> and that we can see have enormous potential.

---

## 5. Production financing

> Selznick reads a script and tells us what it will cost to make: how
> many locations, how big the cast is, night shoots, period detail,
> stunts, how much has to be built or created digitally. It also tells
> us what each of those does to the box office. The same parts of a
> story that drive the cost drive the performance, and Selznick measures
> both at the same time. That is what makes us a real partner to a
> writer. Going through successive drafts with them, we can make every
> decision knowing what it costs and what it earns, so the director's
> vision is properly funded and the film is still made profitably.

Figure: the same film at two budgets. The budget cut ($30M, returns
$180M) against the director's vision made ($45M, returns $420M).

## 6. Cast and crew

> Who makes a film is one of the biggest factors in what it earns, and
> Selznick treats it that way. It has studied the performance history of
> people across the entire entertainment industry in more detail than
> anyone else, including many who are never on the radar of casting
> agents in Hollywood. Selznick uses those histories to determine the
> box office potential of a project. It tells us how much a specific
> person adds to this specific film, and what they cost. It also
> identifies the cast and crew who, in combination, given their
> particular backgrounds and capabilities, are most likely to execute
> this project well and deliver a big hit.

## 7. Vocabulary

**Use:** projects, project (verb), financial performance, market
potential, box office potential, incremental value, precision, appetite,
material, underlying rights, a completed film, cast and crew, vendors
and specialists, the director's vision.

**Never use:** unlock, journey, seamless, empower, leverage (as a verb),
game-changing, revolutionary, "at the end of the day", "that's why we
can", "the days of X are over", "what it takes to get there", "in
service of", "we're excited to".
