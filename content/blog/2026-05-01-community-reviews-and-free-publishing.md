Another big update. Three changes that together make GEM feel a lot more like a real place where writers can grow.

## 1. Community reviews on every script

Every public script on GEM can now be reviewed by other GEM writers. Score from 0 to 100, leave detailed notes, suggest a sharper move for the next draft. You can also invite specific writers — friends, mentors, your writers' room — to review a script, even if it's not public yet.

Why it matters: the Selznick read gives you one signal — a producer-grade structural breakdown of your script. Community reviews give you a second one — what your peers think. Two independent signals are a lot more useful than one. And when an agent, manager, or producer is scanning your work, a script with strong peer reviews stands out as one worth the read.

A few details writers tend to ask about:

- **You stay in control.** You can hide any review from your report (your own page, your call). Hidden reviews don't count toward your peer average and don't show on your script card.
- **Reviews live on the reviewer's profile too.** Good reviews build the reviewer's reputation as much as they help the writer.
- **No drive-by drama.** Reviews are tied to real GEM accounts with public profiles. If the community is going to mean something, the people in it have to mean something.

## 2. Free writers can now publish on GEM

We've always wanted GEM to be a place writers can use for free. Now we really mean it.

If you're on the free plan, you can:

- Publish your script publicly on GEM
- Get peer reviews from other writers
- Build a public profile that other writers and industry partners can find

What's still Pro: **direct industry contact** (producers, agents, managers reach out about specific scripts) and **multiple scripts** (free is one script at a time). Those are the two things Pro unlocks. Everything else — the read, peer reviews, your public profile, the community — is free.

## 3. Simpler privacy

Privacy used to be a per-section toggle. It was thorough and kind of a lot. We've simplified it down to **three account-level defaults** that apply to every script you post:

- **Auto-publish new scripts** — should new scripts go public the moment they're scored, or stay private until you flip them on?
- **Allow reviews** — should other GEM writers be able to read and review your work?
- **Allow industry access** — should producers, agents, and managers be able to read your script and reach out?

You can change any of these at the script level too — every report has a privacy menu in the top-right where you can override the defaults for that one script. And if you change a default later, we'll ask whether you want to apply the change to all your existing scripts or just to new ones from now on.
