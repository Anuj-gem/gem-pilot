Selznick.

Selznick breaks down the narrative, studies massive amounts of market data, and studies the role that individual people can play in maximizing the success of any project. That is completely unprecedented, and it allows us to make smarter investment and creative decisions at every single stage of a project, and turn ideas into massive hits.

Every story ever made

Selznick has read every story the business has ever told and broken each one into the elements that actually move an audience. It reads new material the same way, whether that material is a screenplay, a book, a true story, or an idea nobody has written yet.

A century of box office

Selznick knows what audiences actually paid for, worldwide, across a century of film, and what they watch on television and social today. It compares a project against the entire market rather than against the handful of comparable titles a studio happens to pick.

Every filmmaker and performer

Selznick knows every filmmaker and performer as themselves rather than as an average: what each one has delivered film by film, on this kind of material, at this kind of budget, and alongside these particular people.

Apart, each of these exists somewhere in this business. Together, on one project, they exist nowhere else. That is the difference between an opinion about a film and a price on it.

IP acquisition.

Everything that comes to us is read. Screenplays, books, comics, articles, true stories, finished films, and ideas nobody has written yet. Selznick prices each one in every form it could take and at every budget it could be made for, before a dollar is spent on it. That is why we can move on material the rest of the market has no way to price.

Production financing.

The job is spending money to reach the filmmaker’s vision while the film stays sustainable. Selznick prices the specific risks a director wants to take before they are taken: the location instead of the stage, the practical build instead of the digital one, the extra days on the sequence everyone says is too expensive. The conversation stops being whether it is affordable in the abstract and becomes which risks earn their place.

Cast and crew.

We find people on what they have done, how they work, and who they do their best work with, across every performer and filmmaker rather than the short list already circling a project. Selznick judges what each one adds to this specific story, how they execute together, and what that contribution is worth against the cost of attaching them.

From the founder

Entertainment is in my blood. My dad spent 27 years at Disney. I grew up going to Disneyland several times a year and watching every film they made, and I interned at Walt Disney Studios in college. Then I spent my career building technology and AI at some of the world’s leading technology companies.

This industry has been pointing that technology at the wrong thing. Everyone is trying to make films cheaper. We use it to make them better, in a way no other company can or will, and a film that is ten percent better is worth more than any amount ever saved on one.

Parts of this business are having a hard time right now. What I see is the most abundant moment entertainment has ever had, with more ways for a great story to reach an audience than at any point in history. We are wide open, anyone can bring us an idea, and we have a direct relationship with fans that no studio can match. We are here to build the greatest entertainment company of all time.

— Anuj Kommareddy, founder (LinkedIn)
