Bring us a project Why ↓

Hollywood is stuck in the 20th century.

The market has changed. There are more ways than ever for a great idea to win: more screens, more channels, more paths to a global audience. There are also more ways than ever to lose, and making films has never carried more risk.

In that environment, Hollywood still sizes up risk and opportunity the way David O. Selznick and the great executives of the early 1900s did: instinct, relationships, and a handful of comparable titles. The methods have not changed in a hundred years while the market has changed completely. Faced with risk they cannot measure, studios retreat to what feels safe: franchises, sequels, and remakes. Original films disappeared from theaters not because audiences stopped wanting them, but because nobody in Hollywood is able to take that risk anymore.

Taking a flyer on a great idea has always been part of this business, and Hollywood has never actually gotten better at it. We have figured out how to solve that problem for good, and we intend to build the biggest media company in the world on the back of it.

Our technology: Selznick.

Selznick is the most powerful AI creative partner ever built. It has studied more films, more box office, and more careers than anything else in the industry, and it projects performance and informs decisions with a precision nobody else has. Here is what it lets us do that Hollywood cannot.

We find the ideas Hollywood is not even looking at.

Hollywood is a closed system. A studio reads a few hundred scripts a year, all of them from its own network, all of them by hand. The original thinkers, the daring visions, the ideas that do not look like anything already made: a studio has no way to find those people and no way to judge what they are worth. With Selznick we consider thousands of times more ideas, from sources a studio would never look at, and we know which of them can be enormous. That comes from our technology and from our own time making things for modern audiences, which is what tells us when an idea has real potential and when it only looks like it does.

We make more advanced financial projections than any studio in the world.

From the script alone, before anyone is attached, Selznick projects the full range of outcomes for a film, downside to upside, domestic and international, market by market. Every decision after that sharpens it: a new draft, a lead, a director, the crew, the budget, the release. Each one moves the number and narrows the range. By the time real money is committed, we know more about that film than anyone in Hollywood has ever known about a film before it was made, and that is why we can take risks a studio cannot.

We cast on fit, not fame.

Hollywood casts from a short list of famous names that agents put forward. Selznick knows the full career of every actor, director, writer, and key crew member in the industry: every credit, what each delivered on what material and at what budget, who they do their best work with, how much attention they carry today. For any role in any film it names the people in the world best suited to it and what each would add to the box office. Many of them are on no casting agent’s list, and that is how a director finds exactly the right person for their vision, whatever their fame.

We put every dollar where the audience will feel it.

Hollywood budgets by precedent and then cuts. Selznick reads a script for what it will cost, line by line: locations, cast size, night shoots, period detail, stunts, what has to be built or created digitally. It also knows what each of those lines does to the box office, because the parts of a story that drive the cost are the parts that drive the audience. We put the money where an audience will feel it, we fund the director’s vision instead of cutting it, and the film still returns.

We give filmmakers total creative control.

We invest in filmmakers whose vision we believe in, and then we stay out of the creative decisions. We decide what to finance and at what budget. They decide what the film is, with access to Selznick and the same numbers we have: what a new draft does to the film, what an actor adds, what a location or another shooting day buys on screen. Drafts, casting, budget, they make those calls. No studio can offer this, because no studio has the information to give, so every note becomes an argument between people guessing. We give filmmakers the information and the room, and ambition and a sound investment stop being in conflict.

We make the financial decisions with Selznick in front of us. The filmmakers make the creative decisions with the same thing in front of them.
