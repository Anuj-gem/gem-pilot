The thesis

Great new franchises are getting rarer.

The industry refreshes the same properties again and again — not out of laziness, but because identifying and developing genuinely great new ones is extremely hard.

Why entertainment is in a creative rut.

Entertainment is a business of rare, enormous wins — the top 10% of films earn 72% of all box office. Everything depends on spotting those few. But the industry is structurally blind in three ways, and the biggest ideas keep getting missed because of it.

Closed-off decision makers

The people who can actually make something happen are difficult to reach, and they work with who they've worked with before. The same ideas get recycled, and enormous amounts of talent and IP are never considered at all.

Narrow lenses

The opportunities for a piece of IP have never been greater, but seeing them takes a broad view and real fluency across today's channels. Judged through one lens, most of a property's potential stays invisible — and so does the case for investing in it.

Running on feel

Creative instinct can never be replaced — but the media landscape has become far too complex for instinct alone. Without rigorous research and precise financial modeling, even the best judgment gets misled.

Most of the industry's answer has been to use AI to cut costs — replacing people, automating the work. We don't believe in that. The greatest content will always have people at the center, and the right system makes extraordinary creators and their IP into something even greater.
